Coming of Age and Moral Responsibility

When does understanding create a duty to act?

Essential question

When does understanding create a duty to act?

From topic to theme

Topic: growing up. A topic names an area of interest; a theme statement argues what the novel suggests about it. A defensible statement allows evidence and qualification. It is not an instruction everyone must obey.

Full-novel analysis is hidden. Choose “Full novel” above when you are ready. Chapter-by-chapter notes remain available in the reading guide.

Two defensible arguments

  • Moral maturity involves testing inherited expectations against consequences, even when doing so threatens a person’s belonging.
  • Responsibility can require either leaving a harmful system or staying to help change it; the ethical value lies in the purpose and consequences of the choice.

These interpretations are starting points, not the novel’s official answers.

How the theme develops

Jonas moves from trusting adult decisions to evaluating their effects. Pain and emotional experience make familiar behaviour look different. The Giver and Jonas eventually choose different roles in a shared attempt at change.

Model analytical paragraph

The Giver and Jonas show that responsibility cannot be measured by a single outward action. In Chapter 20, Jonas wants his teacher to leave with him, but the Giver decides to remain and help the community face returned memories. His choice differs from passive obedience because its purpose is to support change. Jonas’s departure serves another need: returning memories and, when events accelerate, protecting Gabriel (Ch. 21). Placing these decisions together prevents a simple claim that leaving is always courageous and staying always cowardly. The novel instead asks readers to judge what a decision responds to, whom it protects, and what burdens the person is prepared to accept.

Original teaching model using paraphrases. Chapter references locate events for study; add page citations from your edition before submitting formal work.

Discuss in increasing depth

  1. Why does Jonas initially rely on his parents’ reassurance?
  2. What changes between discomfort with the rules and a decision to act?
  3. How can the Giver’s decision to stay and Jonas’s decision to leave both express responsibility?

Scaffolded paragraph

Compare two choices. State each purpose, support it with a chapter event, and explain how the comparison complicates an easy judgement about courage.

Use: claim → accurate evidence → explanation of a technique or choice → connection to the theme. Include a qualification such as “although,” “while,” or “does not necessarily.”

Independent writing

Is Jonas’s coming of age primarily a change in knowledge, feeling, or action? Explain their relationship rather than selecting one without qualification.

A common misconception

Growing older does not automatically produce wisdom. Do not assume action is responsible just because it breaks a rule.
