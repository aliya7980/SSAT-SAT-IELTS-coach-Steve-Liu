Sources

Use the novel as evidence, and know what each supporting source can establish.

Primary source: the novel

Lowry, Lois. The Giver. Use the edition assigned in class. Record its publisher, edition statement if applicable, and publication year from the book itself. This site does not assign one universal set of page numbers.

Penguin Random House Higher Education: The Giver, Chapter 1 excerpt — authorized Chapter 1 excerpt used to verify the opening and the short quotation in the writing workshop. The linked product is an audiobook; its bibliographic details are not a template for your print edition.

Secondary study guides

  • SparkNotes: The Giver Study Guide — plot and interpretation cross-checks.
  • SparkNotes: Sparklet Chapter Summaries and the linked grouped chapter analyses.
  • CliffsNotes: Summary and Analysis, Chapters 1–2 — use its contents menu for the other chapter groups.

Grouped chapter guides were consulted across the novel. Their interpretations are secondary perspectives, not statements of authorial intention. All explanations, summaries, models, activities, and design here are original teaching material. No affiliation or endorsement is implied.

Curriculum and assessment

  • BC Curriculum: English Language Arts 8 — connects reading strategies, interpretation, literary elements, communication, and revision.
  • VSB, Prince of Wales Secondary: Assessment and Reporting — context for Grade 8–9 proficiency reporting and self-assessment.
  • VSB, David Thompson Secondary: Proficiency Scale Explained.
  • BC Ministry: Unpacking the Proficiency Scale—Support for Educators.

The four-criterion unit rubric is a teacher-created adaptation, not an official VSB assessment document. It assigns no official percentage conversions.

Official MLA guidance

  • MLA Style Center: In-Text Citations—An Overview.
  • MLA Style Center: Integrating Quotations in Research Writing: A Lesson for College Students — an older teaching resource marked as eighth-edition material; read alongside the current overview.
  • MLA Style Center: If my paraphrase consists of several sentences, should a citation for the original source appear after each sentence? — an older post explicitly marked as relating to the eighth edition; use alongside current ninth-edition overview guidance.
  • MLA Style Center: Citations by Format — Works Cited entries based on applicable core elements.

Verification and limits

Sources checked 2 November 2026. Publicly accessible SparkNotes and CliffsNotes sections were available, though SparkNotes also displays subscription prompts. BC curriculum, VSB, and MLA pages were accessible. The Ministry proficiency PDF returned a temporary 502 on direct retrieval during this revision; its indexed official text was available and checked. Only the authorized opening excerpt was available as primary text during preparation; a complete authorized copy was not available in this workspace. Chapter events were cross-checked against secondary guides, but full primary-text verification of every chapter remains a teacher review task before classroom adoption.

The only novel quotation here is the short opening phrase verified in the publisher excerpt. Other novel evidence is paraphrased. Teacher-written descriptive examples are clearly labelled. No page numbers, edition details, or authorial intentions have been invented. Interpretations are presented as arguable readings, not facts about what Lowry intended.

How evidence is used in this course

All chapter summaries, event examples, and model paragraphs are original paraphrases except the identified four-word opening phrase. The primary excerpt verifies that phrase and the opening scene. The remainder is event-based analysis cross-checked through the accessible grouped chapter guides. Exact wording beyond the excerpt has not been authenticated against a full primary text, so it is not presented as quotation.

The site distinguishes Chapter 19’s revelation from Chapter 21’s decisive departure and Chapter 23’s physical crisis. It keeps the novel’s ending unresolved and does not import the film’s expanded Fiona storyline or a sequel’s answers. Interpretations about motives are qualified where limited narration does not supply private thoughts.

Teacher answer reveals are a display feature. The print control excludes them by default, even if a reveal was opened on screen. Selecting “Include teacher answers in print” deliberately includes answers for the chapters or lessons currently available under the spoiler settings.
