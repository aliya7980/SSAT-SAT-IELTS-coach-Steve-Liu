Style and Techniques

Name a method only after you can explain what it does.

How readers learn beside Jonas

The novel uses third-person limited narration centred on Jonas: it refers to him in the third person but primarily gives access to his thoughts and perceptions. Readers receive explanations as he encounters them. A narrator’s report of what Jonas thinks is not proof that his interpretation is complete.

Figurative language is a narrower category

A simile makes an explicit comparison, often using “like” or “as.” A metaphor describes one thing in terms of another. Personification gives human qualities to something nonhuman. Narration, pacing, irony, and foreshadowing are broader techniques, not automatically figures of speech.

This site does not label unverified lines from the novel as metaphors or similes. Find a candidate in your own copy, record its exact wording and page, and explain the comparison before naming it. Not every vivid description is figurative language.

Compare two original teaching examples

These passages are teacher-written examples, not quotations from Lowry.

Neutral descriptionDescriptive passage
The corridor was quiet. A bell rang. Mara stopped at the door.The bell cut through the corridor’s silence like a snapped wire. Mara’s hand hovered above the handle.

The second passage uses a simile to make the sound feel sharp, a metaphorical verb (“cut”), and a precise physical action that suggests hesitation. It does not explain Mara’s feelings directly.

Discuss: What inference can you support about Mara, and what remains unknown?

Write: Describe the same doorway as welcoming. Change sensory details and diction while keeping the basic event.
