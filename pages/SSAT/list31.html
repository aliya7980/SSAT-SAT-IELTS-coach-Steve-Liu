SSAT Vocabulary Practice: List 31

Twenty-word practice set from the Ubiquitous Essentials SSAT/SAT academic glossary.

Vocabulary List 31: DO-EM Words

1. DocileSSAT vocabularyEasily taught, led, or managed.
2. DoctrinaireSSAT vocabularyStubbornly devoted to a theory regardless of practicality.
3. DogmaticSSAT vocabularyAssertive of opinions as if they were unquestionable facts.
4. DomesticSSAT vocabularyRelated to home, family, or one's own country.
5. DormantSSAT vocabularyInactive but capable of becoming active.
6. DubiousSSAT vocabularyDoubtful or uncertain.
7. DuplicitySSAT vocabularyDeceitfulness or double-dealing.
8. EbullientSSAT vocabularyCheerful, enthusiastic, and full of energy.
9. EccentricSSAT vocabularyUnconventional or oddly different.
10. EclecticSSAT vocabularyDrawn from a variety of sources or styles.
11. EdifySSAT vocabularyTo instruct or improve morally or intellectually.
12. EffaceSSAT vocabularyTo erase, wipe out, or make less noticeable.
13. EffusionSSAT vocabularyAn unrestrained expression of feeling.
14. EgalitarianSSAT vocabularyBelieving in equal rights and opportunities.
15. EgocentricSSAT vocabularySelf-centered; seeing everything from one's own view.
16. EgregiousSSAT vocabularyOutstandingly bad or shocking.
17. ElicitSSAT vocabularyTo draw out a response or information.
18. EllipticalSSAT vocabularyIndirect, obscure, or leaving words out.
19. ElusiveSSAT vocabularyHard to catch, find, or understand.
20. EmigrateSSAT vocabularyTo leave one's country to live elsewhere.

Exercise 3: Paragraph Using All Words

Read the paragraph and notice how each vocabulary word is used in context.

In one busy school week, every conversation seemed to introduce a new vocabulary word. The solution remained Elusive until the last clue appeared. The excuse sounded Dubious because no one could confirm it. The volcano looked quiet but remained Dormant beneath the snow. The playlist was Eclectic, mixing jazz, opera, and hip-hop. Her Duplicity was exposed when both versions of the story reached the teacher. The article compared foreign policy with Domestic issues such as schools and roads. Her thank-you note was an Effusion of relief and joy. The coach used the mistake to Edify the whole team. The club adopted an Egalitarian rule so every member had one vote. The lab report had an Egregious error: it listed the wrong chemical. His Eccentric study method involved drawing cartoons for every history term. The teacher asked an open question to Elicit thoughtful answers. The Egocentric narrator described every event as if it revolved around him. The leader sounded Doctrinaire when he ignored every practical objection. The Ebullient host made every nervous guest feel welcome. The rain began to Efface the chalk notes on the sidewalk. His grandparents decided to Emigrate during a time of political unrest. The speaker became Dogmatic, refusing to admit that others might be right. The calm puppy was Docile during training class. His reply was so Elliptical that nobody knew whether he agreed.
