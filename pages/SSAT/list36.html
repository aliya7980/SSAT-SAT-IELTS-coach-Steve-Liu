SSAT Vocabulary Practice: List 36

Twenty-word practice set from the Ubiquitous Essentials SSAT/SAT academic glossary.

Vocabulary List 36: GE-HE Words

1. GesticulateSSAT vocabularyTo make expressive gestures while speaking.
2. GlutSSAT vocabularyAn excessive supply; to overfill.
3. DearthSSAT vocabularyA scarcity or serious lack of something.
4. GrandiloquentSSAT vocabularyPompous or overly lofty in speech.
5. PompousSSAT vocabularySelf-important and overly grand.
6. GrandioseSSAT vocabularyOverly ambitious, impressive, or exaggerated.
7. GratuitousSSAT vocabularyUncalled for or given freely without cause.
8. GratuitySSAT vocabularyA tip or gift of money for service.
9. GravitySSAT vocabularySeriousness; or physical force pulling objects together.
10. GregariousSSAT vocabularySociable and fond of company.
11. GuileSSAT vocabularyClever deceit or trickery.
12. ArtfulnessSSAT vocabularyClever skill, often with craftiness.
13. HackneyedSSAT vocabularyOverused and lacking freshness.
14. TriteSSAT vocabularyOverused and lacking originality.
15. HaplessSSAT vocabularyUnlucky or unfortunate.
16. HarbingerSSAT vocabularyA sign or messenger of what is to come.
17. ForerunnerSSAT vocabularySomething or someone that comes before and signals another.
18. HedonismSSAT vocabularyThe pursuit of pleasure as a chief goal.
19. HegemonySSAT vocabularyDominance or leadership by one group over others.
20. HeresySSAT vocabularyAn opinion contrary to accepted belief.

Exercise 3: Paragraph Using All Words

Read the paragraph and notice how each vocabulary word is used in context.

In one busy school week, every conversation seemed to introduce a new vocabulary word. The novel criticizes Hedonism when comfort becomes the only goal. The market had a Glut of apples after the harvest. The first cough was a Forerunner of a long cold. The Hapless camper forgot a raincoat during the storm. The movie included a Gratuitous insult that added nothing to the story. The waiter thanked the family for leaving a generous Gratuity. The captain sounded Pompous when he called himself the only reason the team won. The trick worked because of the thief's Guile. When excited, the actor would Gesticulate with both hands. The poster showed Artfulness in the way every color guided the viewer's eye. Dark clouds were a Harbinger of the coming storm. There was a Dearth of pencils on test day. The phrase "follow your dreams" sounded Trite without a fresh example. The Gregarious student made friends on the first day. The warning carried enough Gravity that everyone listened. The phrase "better late than never" felt Hackneyed in the essay. The speech sounded Grandiloquent, as if the student were trying to impress judges with fancy words. The plan to build a theater in one weekend was Grandiose. At first, saying Earth moved around the sun was treated as Heresy. The empire maintained Hegemony over neighboring states.
